About

I'm an Engineering Manager at NVIDIA working on Physical AI and Simulation. My teams build the reinforcement learning environments and high-fidelity simulators that teach robots to perceive, reason, and act in the physical world, then close the loop from simulation to real hardware.

I lead multidisciplinary teams across the full stack, from low-level hardware and controls to the software, physics engines, and RL training infrastructure that sit on top. I care about the seams where these disciplines meet, because that is usually where the hardest and most interesting problems in robotics and simulation live.

Before NVIDIA I spent much of my career in aerospace simulation and large-scale software. At Relativity Space I built rocket and real-time 3D physics simulators in C, C++, and Python; at SpaceX I worked on spacecraft command software; and at Google I built data-processing systems at scale. That mix of physical systems and heavy software is exactly the ground I like to build teams on.

FPGA Maze Game 2016

Verilog · FPGA · LED Matrix · Digital Logic

A maze game implemented entirely in hardware on an FPGA, written in Verilog. I built a physical LED matrix that served as the actual display — there was no VGA or monitor, the game rendered directly onto the LEDs I wired up. The FPGA drove the game logic, the matrix multiplexing, and player input handling, all as synchronous digital logic rather than software.

EHD (Ion) Thruster 2015

High Voltage · Propulsion · Physics

An electrohydrodynamic (ion) thruster that produces thrust from ionic wind with no moving parts. A high-voltage potential between an emitter and collector ionizes air and accelerates the ions, dragging neutral air along with them. The project explored the electrode geometry, the high-voltage supply, and the thrust-to-power tradeoffs of the effect.
